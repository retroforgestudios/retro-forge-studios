A lot of small business owners around Omaha, and across Nebraska and Iowa, check
their Instagram or Facebook page, see the follower count creep up, and figure
things are working. Then they look at the bank account and can't draw a straight
line between the two.

You're not imagining it. Organic engagement on the big platforms is genuinely low
right now — Facebook posts average around a 0.15% engagement rate and Instagram
sits around 0.48%, according to Sprout Social's 2026 benchmarks. So even a page
with a few thousand followers might only get real interaction from a few dozen
people per post. If you're judging your marketing by likes and follower growth
alone, you're grading yourself on a curve that has almost nothing to do with
revenue.

Followers and likes are what's called a "vanity metric" — easy to see, easy to
feel good about, and mostly disconnected from whether anyone actually buys from
you. Here's what to track instead, and how to do it without hiring an analytics
team.

## 1. Link clicks, not likes

Every post you make should usually be trying to get someone to do something —
visit your website, call, book, or walk in. Track how many people actually click
the link in your bio or post, not how many hearted it. Most platforms show this
for free in their built-in insights tab.

## 2. Website traffic from social

If you have Google Analytics (or even the free Google Business Profile insights),
check how many visits are coming from Facebook, Instagram, or TikTok specifically.
That's a much stronger signal than engagement, because it means someone left the
app to come look at your business.

## 3. Saves and shares over likes

A "like" takes zero effort and means almost nothing. A save or a share means
someone thought "I need this" or "so-and-so needs to see this" — that's a much
closer cousin to actual buying intent. Most platforms report saves separately
from likes in the post-level insights.

## 4. DMs and comments that turn into real conversations

If someone comments "how much?" or DMs you about booking, that's a lead, full
stop. Keep a simple running count of these each week — a sticky note or one line
in a spreadsheet is enough. Over a month you'll start to see which types of posts
actually generate them.

## 5. "How'd you hear about us?"

Nothing beats just asking. When a new customer calls or comes in, ask them how
they found you and jot it down. After a month of doing this consistently, you'll
have a far more honest picture of what's driving business than any dashboard will
give you — because it's tied to actual dollars, not app-reported numbers.

**Quick fix:** You don't need expensive software to track any of this. A simple
spreadsheet with five columns — platform, post, link clicks, saves/shares, and
inquiries — updated once a week takes ten minutes and will tell you more about
your marketing than a stack of follower-count screenshots ever could.

## The honest part

Social media should be judged the same way you'd judge any other marketing spend
— by whether it moves people toward becoming customers, not by how it makes your
page look. Once you start tracking the numbers that actually matter, you'll
usually find that a couple of your posts are doing all the heavy lifting, and a
lot of the rest is just noise. That's useful information — it tells you exactly
where to spend less time and where to double down.
